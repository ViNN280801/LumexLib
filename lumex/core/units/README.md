# LumexUnits: a catalog of units of measurement {#lumex_units}

`lumex::core::units` (target `lumex::units`, header-only, from C++11) is a table of units of measurement with their symbols in two scripts, the 24 SI prefixes, exact conversion factors, the lookup of a symbol and the conversion of a `double`. It is a catalog and not a library of typed quantities: there is no `quantity<unit, double>` and no compound-unit grammar (`mg/dm3`); the callers compose compound units themselves.

```cpp
// lumex/examples/units/example_units.cpp (shortened)
#include "lumex/core/units/LumexUnits"

using namespace lumex::core::units;

result_t<prefixed_unit_t, lookup_error> unit = lookup (text_view_t ("kPa"));   // pascal, prefix kilo
prefixed_unit_t const milligram = { unit_id::gram, prefix_id::milli };
prefixed_unit_t const gram = { unit_id::gram, prefix_id::none };
double const grams = convert (700.0, milligram, gram).value ();               // exactly the double 0.7
```

## What is in the catalog

105 units, each with one row in `data/LumexUnitsData.hpp` and the comment that names its source: the seven SI base units, the 22 SI units with special names, the non-SI units that GOST 8.417-2024 allows on a par with the SI (table 5), the relative and logarithmic units (table 6), the units allowed temporarily (table 7), the old units of annex G of the GOST (the CGS units, the kilogram-force, the calorie, the curie, the roentgen ...) and the standard atmosphere (10th CGPM, 1954). Information units (bit, byte) and imperial units beyond the foot and the nautical mile are not in it.

| Part | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `unit_id` | One enumerator per unit, spelled in English (metre, litre, tonne; BIPM spelling) |
| `unit_descriptor_t` | The domain (a part of ISO/IEC 80000), the quantity kind, where the unit stands in the standards (`standing`), the system (SI, non-SI, CGS), both canonical symbols, the dimension, the value in the coherent SI unit, the offset, the factor kind, the prefix set and whether the symbol is a named compound |
| `prefix_id`, `prefix_descriptor_t` | The prefixes quecto to quetta with both symbols; the prefix names of the 27th CGPM (2022) have the Russian symbols of the 2024 standard |
| `LUMEX_UNITS_CATALOG (X)` and the other X-macros | The rows, the aliases, the prefixes and the shadowed symbols, for a consumer that builds its own tables |

The international symbols follow the SI Brochure (9th edition, version 4.01), the Russian ones GOST 8.417-2024; where the two differ the other form is an accepted alias (`au` and `ua`, `G` and `Gs`). The units that are measured and not defined (the dalton, the X unit) have `factor_kind::measured`, the logarithmic units `factor_kind::none` (no value in an SI unit).

## The value of a unit

The value of a unit in the coherent SI unit of its dimension (the kilogram for mass) is stored as $$10^{k} \cdot \frac{p}{q} \cdot \pi^{n}$$ with the decimal exponent $$k$$ (32-bit), the numerator $$p$$ and the denominator $$q$$ (64-bit, coprime, no factor 10 left) and the power $$n$$ of the number pi. This holds every defined factor of the catalog exactly (1 eV = 801088317 / 5 * 10^-27 J, 1 mmHg = 26664477483 / 2 * 10^-8 Pa, 1 cal = 10467 / 25 * 10^-2 J); `std::ratio` cannot (it stops at 10^18 and has no pi). The degree, the minute and the second of arc, the gon, the revolution, the square degree, the oersted and the gilbert contain pi and are not rational; a conversion of them is, like every other one, the correctly rounded value of the exact product (see below).

The gram, not the kilogram, is the catalog unit of mass, so that the prefixes attach to `g`; the kilogram is the pair (`prefix_id::kilo`, `unit_id::gram`) and has the scale 1.

## Lookup, formatting, ratio and conversion

| Function | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `lookup (text, script)`, `lookup (text)` | A bare symbol, an alias, or a prefix and a symbol, in one script or in either; `expected<prefixed_unit_t, lookup_error>`. Under `LUMEX_HAS_CHAR8_T` the same with a `char8_t` view, and `canonical_symbol_u8`, `symbol_buffer_t::view_u8` |
| `format_symbol (pair, script)` | The canonical symbol of a prefix and a unit in a fixed buffer (`symbol_buffer_t`); a pair that another unit's bare symbol shadows is refused with `format_error::shadowed`, so what is written is always read back as the same pair |
| `canonical_symbol`, `descriptor`, `scale_of`, `takes_prefix`, `is_affine` | Access to the data |
| `ratio (from, to)` | The exact factor as a canonical scale (checked 64-bit arithmetic, reduced by the common divisors before multiplying; `overflow` instead of wrapping) |
| `convert (x, from, to)` | `x` times the double-double factor of `ratio` (`make_converter` and `apply`), correctly rounded, for linear units of one quantity kind |
| `make_converter (from, to)`, `converter_t::apply (x)` | The checks (kinds, affine units, arguments) and the exact factor, done once; `apply` converts a value in a few nanoseconds. `make_converter (scale)` makes one from a scale |
| `scale_factor (scale)`, `apply_scale (scale, x)` | The factor of a scale as the nearest double (the `high` part), and `x` times the double-double factor |
| `convert_point`, `convert_difference` | The degree Celsius (and the kelvin) as a point with the exact offset 273.15 K, and as a difference |

Rules of the lookup, each pinned by a test:

- A bare symbol wins over a generated pair: the Russian `Тл` is the tesla, not the teralitre. The twelve shadowed pairs (the symbols of the gauss `Гс`, `Gs`, the gram-force, the gilbert, the arcsecond `as`, the foot, the nit, the gon) are in `LUMEX_UNITS_SHADOWED`, which a test recomputes from the catalog; they are reached by their `unit_id` and `prefix_id`.
- There are no compound prefixes, and the prefix goes on one symbol of a unit that takes it: `µkg` is unknown. The units of time other than the second, the units of plane angle (but the arcsecond `as`: milli, micro and pico), the astronomical unit, the diopter, the atomic mass unit in Russian and `u` take no prefixes.
- The international canonical symbols follow the order of the sources: `mm Hg` (GOST table 7) and `Gs` for the gauss; `mmHg` and `G` are accepted. The metric horsepower is exactly 735.49875 W and the parsec exactly 648000 / pi au (IAU 2015 B2); the degree Celsius and the gon take prefixes. The micro sign U+00B5 is the output; U+03BC and the letter `u` are accepted. The ohm is U+03A9; U+2126 and `ohm` are accepted. The angstrom U+00C5 and the kelvin K accept their compatibility signs (U+212B, U+212A), the degree Celsius U+2103. The Cyrillic letter in `°С` is refused, also after a prefix.
- A text that mixes the scripts (`мкg`) is unknown, with one exception: a Cyrillic prefix with the Latin degree Celsius (`м°C`, the milli in Cyrillic) is accepted in the Russian script, because the Russian column of the standard writes the Latin C. A Cyrillic C after the degree sign stays unknown (`°С`, `м°С`).
- Some spellings are input aliases that are never written: the Russian words `атм` (standard atmosphere) and `торр` (torr), for which no standard read (GOST 8.417-2024 table 7 and annex G, GOST 8.417-2002 annex B) gives a Russian symbol, the form `мм. рт. ст` of the online text of table 7, and the ASCII `'` and `"` of the arc minute and second (no prefixes). `format_symbol` writes a unit only from its canonical symbol (or, with a prefix, from an alias that takes prefixes when the canonical symbol does not); a unit that has no canonical symbol in a script has none to write there. The kilopond and the pond have no Russian symbol and no alias: a bare letter would make the lookup of that letter surprising. The dose rad has `rd` and no Russian symbol, its Russian name is the symbol of the radian.
- The oersted and the gilbert take all 24 prefixes (`kOe`, `mGb`); the alias `Gi` of the gilbert takes none, because `Gi` is also the binary prefix gibi.
- The headers and the umbrella are ASCII (symbols are `\x` escapes, comments name a character as U+XXXX), so a unit compiles the same with and without `/utf-8` on any code page; the CTest `lint.units_ascii` keeps it so.
- Only units of one quantity kind convert: the hertz does not convert to the becquerel, the gray to the sievert, the revolution per second to the hertz. A unit without a value (bel, decibel, neper, phon, octave, decade) never converts.

A conversion is a double-double product. The factor of a pair of units is the exact $$10^{k} \cdot \frac{p}{q} \cdot \pi^{n}$$, computed in integers of up to 3584 bits (pi with 126 bits after the point) and kept as two doubles `high + low`: `high` is the nearest double, `low` the nearest double of the exact difference, so the pair holds about 106 bits. The value is converted by one of three routes (`LUMEX_UNITS_ROUTE`, chosen by the compiler flags, see the table):

| Route | The product | Taken when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| fused (`LUMEX_UNITS_ROUTE_FUSED`) | `fma (x, high, x * low)`, the product exact inside the hardware instruction | `FP_FAST_FMA`, `__FP_FAST_FMA` or `__FMA__` is defined (`-mfma`, `-march=x86-64-v3`), MSVC with `/arch:AVX2`, ARM64 |
| two-product (`LUMEX_UNITS_ROUTE_TWO_PRODUCT`) | Dekker's two-product: `x * high` as an exact sum of two doubles, then `RN (product + RN (error + RN (x * low)))` | exact binary64 arithmetic and no hardware fused operation: the default build of x86-64 with GCC, Clang, cl and clang-cl, MinGW-w64 x86-64 without `-mfma` (its `std::fma` is not fused and is not called); also with `-funsafe-math-optimizations` on GCC 9 to 13 and on clang 14, 16 and later, where the cores protect themselves (below) |
| exact (`LUMEX_UNITS_ROUTE_EXACT`) | the product of the integer mantissas, rounded once; hundreds of nanoseconds | `FLT_EVAL_METHOD` is not 0 or not defined (x87, i686 builds), `__FAST_MATH__` (`-ffast-math`), finite-math-only (`__FINITE_MATH_ONLY__` is 1: `-ffinite-math-only`, `-ffast-math`), MSVC and clang-cl `/fp:fast` (`_M_FP_FAST`), clang 15 under `-funsafe-math-optimizations` (its `FLT_EVAL_METHOD` is -1), a clang before 14: the compiler may rewrite the two-product, and excess precision breaks it |

The route is selected by those macros, not by the flag `-ffast-math` as such: `-ffast-math -fno-finite-math-only` defines neither `__FAST_MATH__` nor `__FINITE_MATH_ONLY__` of 1, takes the two-product route (the fused one with `-march=x86-64-v3`) and is correct, because the cores protect themselves (0 differences of about 197 thousand random results on GCC 9, 11 and 13 and clang 14, 16 and 18).

The x87 build (`-mfpmath=387`) and the builds with `-funsafe-math-optimizations` (the suites of the variant `unsafe_math`: GCC 9 to 13, clang 14, 15, 16 and 18) were run; MSVC `/fp:fast` is the same variant under cl.exe and clang-cl. Neither compiler can be asked whether `-fassociative-math` is on (GCC defines `__ASSOCIATIVE_MATH__` only from version 12, clang never; `__FAST_MATH__` is set by `-ffast-math` only), so the flag is not read: the code that must not be reassociated is protected where it stands. The cores of the fused and two-product routes, the split and the scaling switch the reassociation off for themselves: GCC with `__attribute__ ((optimize ("no-associative-math")))`, applied where the flag can be on: before GCC 12 always (there is no macro to ask), from GCC 12 only where `__ASSOCIATIVE_MATH__` is defined (every set of flags that reassociates defines it there, `-funsafe-math-optimizations` and `-fassociative-math -fno-signed-zeros -fno-trapping-math` among them; a program that mixes units built with and without it, with and without LTO, gave 0 wrong results) (every GCC from 9 to 13: 0 wrong of 395712 random results with `-funsafe-math-optimizations` at `-O2`, and at `-O3` with `-march=x86-64-v3` (`-march=haswell` for GCC 9 and 10), on the route of the build and on the forced two-product route; the same flags without the attribute misrounded 35237 of 395712 on every one of them), clang with `#pragma clang fp reassociate(off)` (clang 14 and later). A clang before 14 (no such pragma) takes the exact route, whatever its flags are. Clang 15 under `-funsafe-math-optimizations` has `FLT_EVAL_METHOD` of -1 and takes the exact route by that; clang 14, 16 and 18 keep the route of the target (two-product, fused with `-mfma`). The call of `std::fma` is not under the pragma (clang marks it `reassoc` all the same): a fused core without the instruction, which is a route that the user forced, is rewritten under `-fassociative-math` (about 35 thousand of 395712 on clang 14 to 18), and a forced route is the user's own responsibility under flags that let the compiler reassociate. On GCC 11 and later the attribute keeps `multiply_add_scaled` out of line, which is why it costs: `apply` per value (`-O2`, best of 7, with the attribute and without it) took 2.61 and 2.61 ns on GCC 9, 2.45 and 2.43 ns on GCC 10 and 3.33 and 2.44 ns on GCC 11 at the default flags (GCC 11 has no macro to ask, so it pays); with the gate on `__ASSOCIATIVE_MATH__` GCC 12 and 13 pay nothing there (2.34 and 2.27 ns; 3.30 and 3.13 ns when the attribute was applied everywhere, 2.24 and 2.22 ns without it). Under `-funsafe-math-optimizations` every GCC pays about 1.0 to 1.4 ns (3.83 and 2.84 ns on GCC 9, 3.85 and 2.80 on GCC 10, 3.89 and 2.83 on GCC 11, 3.84 and 2.48 on GCC 12, 3.70 and 2.42 on GCC 13). `LUMEX_UNITS_ROUTE` may be defined before the first include to force a route (the test suites do: the variants `two_product` and `exact` run every suite with it). `LUMEX_UNITS_EXCESS_PRECISION` (1 where `FLT_EVAL_METHOD` is not 0 or not defined, otherwise 0) is a public read-only macro like `LUMEX_UNITS_ROUTE`: it is not to be defined by the user, and the tests read it.

The route is part of the name of an inline namespace (`route_fused`, `route_two_product`, `route_exact`) that holds `converter_t`, everything that calls `apply` and the helpers that the products use (the integers of the factor, the split of the two-product, the three products: `route_detail` inside it). What that gives: units built with different routes (`LUMEX_UNITS_ROUTE` forced differently, or flags of the floating-point model that select different routes) keep their own copies of every function that depends on the route, so they never meet in the ODR, and `LumexUnitsRouteMix` links units of three routes into one executable. What it does not give: all units of a program that include LumexLib must be built for one instruction set, as with any inline C++ code. The other inline functions of the header (`ratio`, `format_symbol`, `lookup`, the `expected` constructors) are the same in every route and the linker keeps one copy of each; a copy that was compiled with VEX instructions (for example `-mfma` or `-mavx2`) can be the one kept for a unit that is built without them, and then stops it with SIGILL on a CPU that has no such instructions.

The two-product does not depend on how the compiler contracts `a * b + c`. A compiler that fuses the Veltkamp split and the last sum of the two-product with the multiplication that gave `product` (GCC with FMA instructions enabled does by default, clang with `-ffp-contract=fast`) counts the error of that product twice: GCC 13 at `-O2 -march=x86-64-v3 -DLUMEX_UNITS_ROUTE=2` misrounded 1715 of 395712 random results of exponents up to +-600 (the exponents of +-100 passed, and so did a build with `-ffp-contract=off`). The route has two defences, and each of them removed the misrounding alone in the builds that were measured: the halves of the split are chosen in the bits of the number (no arithmetic, nothing to contract or to evaluate in excess precision), and the rounded product is stored through a `volatile` object, so no multiplication is fused across the store. Every product of two halves is exact, and fusing `x * low` with the sum rounds it once instead of twice, inside the bound. The sweep of the tests (exponents up to +-600, against the exact route) finds a contracted two-product.

- The result is the correctly rounded value of the exact product, except when that product lies within a bound of a tie between two doubles: 2^-105 = 2.5e-32 (relative) for the fused and the two-product routes, and 2^-106 = 1.2e-32 for the exact route. The exact route rounds `x * (high + low)` once, not `x` times the true factor, and `high + low` differs from the true factor by up to 2^-106 (relative; a search of near ties found 404 failures, all within 5.3e-33), so it has a limit too; the number pi, taken with 126 bits, adds below 1e-37 per power on every route. The fused route has one rounding of the exact `x * high` plus a rounded correction, the two-product route has three roundings of which the first two are of quantities far below the last bit. The tests compare 119960 results of 5998 (unit, prefix) pairs with the correctly rounded values of the oracle bit for bit through five routes (`convert`, `apply`, fused, two-product, exact), with no difference. 700 of a milli unit is exactly the double 0.7.
- The two non-exact routes scale the arguments by exact powers of two so that the magnitude of the product is in [2^-900.24, 2^995.98] (the limits are the literals 1.0e-271 and 6.6e299; 2^995.98 times the 27 bits of the split factor stays below 2^1024): above, the products of the split halves overflow (for a result within 2^-26 of the largest double), below, the error term and `x * low` fall into the subnormal range (for a result below 2^-969). The tests step through both bands, 4096 values below the largest double and 12001 values from 2^-1022 up, and compare every route bit for bit.
- The guarantee holds for a factor above 2^-960 (so that `low` is not subnormal) and a result in the normal range, not below 2^-1022; below it the result is rounded twice. It also needs the denormals and the sign of a zero: `-ffast-math` and `-funsafe-math-optimizations` link the start-up code that clears the denormals (a value below 2^-968 is scaled up by 2^120 before the split, because the lower half of its split is subnormal and DAZ and FTZ flush it: with the scaling, 0 of the random results with a value in [2^-1021, 2^-969) differ on the two-product and the fused routes under DAZ and FTZ, and a test sets both bits in MXCSR and runs that band; a subnormal operand or result stays outside the guarantee, as does a `high` below 2^-960) and `-fno-signed-zeros` lets the compiler drop the sign of a zero result. A value is never taken through an intermediate overflow or underflow, a zero keeps its sign (without those flags), an infinity and a NaN pass like in a multiplication, and a product that overflows is the infinity of its sign, but only when the correctly rounded result does: `x * high` that overflows in a double while the exact product rounds to the largest double gives the largest double (the arguments are run scaled down by 2^-64). On a machine with excess precision (`FLT_EVAL_METHOD` not 0, x87) the exact route forms no product of two doubles (a plain product with `low == 0` is taken in integers too), so a product is rounded once there as well, not to 64 bits and then to 53.
- A factor is computed once per call of `convert` (about 0.5 to 1.2 microseconds on x64, the division of integers of up to 3584 bits); for many values of one pair, make the converter once, then `apply` takes about 1 ns with the fused operation and 3 ns with Dekker's route.
- The integers have a fixed size: the value of a scale is estimated first from its binary logarithm, a value above 2^1100 is infinity and below 2^-1100 is zero whatever its exponents, and nothing larger than the buffer is ever formed. A `|pi_power|` above 16 (`scale_pi_power_limit`; the catalog needs 2) of a value that is in the range gives a NaN factor, and `make_converter (from, to)` then reports `conversion_error::overflow`.

## Millimetre of mercury and the torr

The standards give three numbers: the conventional millimetre of mercury is 13595.1 kg/m3 times 9.80665 m/s2 times 1 mm, 133.322387415 Pa (the table of GOST 8.417-2024 rounds it to 133.3224 Pa); the torr is 1/760 of the standard atmosphere, 101325/760 Pa. They differ by 1.4e-7 (relative), so the catalog has both: `unit_id::millimetre_of_mercury` and `unit_id::torr`.

## Testing

`ctest -R '^units\.'` runs the suites at C++11, 14, 17 and 20 (`units.data.*`: the catalog in constant expressions and every row against the oracle; `units.api.*`: the lookup, the shadowed symbols, the rules of the prefixes, the encodings, the ratio, the conversions of the oracle, the sweep of the double-double factor, the converter, the size of the integers, the refusals and the temperatures); every suite of the module also runs as the variants `two_product` (C++11 and C++20) and `exact` (C++11) with `LUMEX_UNITS_ROUTE` forced, and as `unsafe_math` (C++11), compiled with `-funsafe-math-optimizations` (cl.exe and clang-cl: `/fp:fast`) where the compiler may reassociate; `LumexUnitsRouteMix` links units of different routes into one program. The oracle is `lumex/tests/core/units/oracle/oracle_units.py`, a second transcription of the standards, which also writes the C++ data of the tests; `units.oracle_up_to_date` regenerates it and fails on a difference.
